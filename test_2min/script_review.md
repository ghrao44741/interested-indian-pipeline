BORING_SECTIONS:
1. "The court said it was taken out of context. Nobody online particularly cared — by the next morning, a student had already announced the 'Cockroach Janta Party' as satire."
   WHY: This is Wikipedia-level exposition. The "nobody cared" line tries to be punchy but lands flat because it's telling rather than showing. The satire announcement feels too neutral, like you're just reporting timeline facts.

2. "The government's line, for most of that time, was fix the machine, not the guy who broke it."
   WHY: This metaphor is too abstract. What does "fix the machine" mean concretely? It reads like a political science textbook trying to sound clever. The viewer's brain has to work to translate it.

3. "Then a planned march toward Parliament turned into tear gas and baton charges."
   WHY: This is pure news-report language. "Turned into" is passive and vague. No personality, no angle, no narrator voice — could be from any wire service.

4. "And then the government did the most government thing possible: it announced a brand-new committee to fix the exam system, chaired by the man who built Aadhaar."
   WHY: "The most government thing possible" is trying to be wry but it's too generic. Also, "chaired by the man who built Aadhaar" assumes I know why that's ironic or significant — the jargon rule demands you explain *why* this choice matters immediately.

5. "Both camps have footage. Both camps have hashtags."
   WHY: This parallelism is trying too hard to be poetic. It's rhythmically cute but informationally empty — it tells me nothing about what actually happened or why it matters.

RECOMMENDATIONS:

1. **Fix the Aadhaar reference immediately.** Right now it's insider knowledge. Try: "...chaired by the man who built Aadhaar — India's biometric identity system that also had its own leak scandals — which is either ironic or the only qualification that matters." Give the viewer the context to get the joke.

2. **Restate/complicate the thesis at midpoint.** Around paragraph 3-4, you need to loop back to the absurdity of the Cockroach Party hook. The arc DNA says for comparative/two-entity topics, complicate the thesis mid-script. Here you have "Minister vs. Insect Party" — milk that tension halfway through with something like: "So a party named after a pest was now the government's actual problem."

3. **Replace news-report sentences with narrator perspective.** "Then a planned march toward Parliament turned into tear gas and baton charges" should become something like: "Then a march was planned toward Parliament, and if you've studied Indian protests, you know exactly one way this ends: tear gas, baton charges, and three different versions of who started it."

4. **Make the cockroach judge moment land harder.** The current telling ("made a remark that got read online as...") is too cautious and boring. Show the absurdity: "Then a Supreme Court judge, in an unrelated hearing, used the word 'cockroach' in a sentence about jobless youth. The court later said context matters. Twitter said context is for losers."

5. **Sharpen the close to echo the hook more directly.** The final line about reports is good, but it doesn't circle back to the twelve-year minister or the cockroach party. Try ending with something that ties the insect metaphor to the bureaucratic cycle: "The Cockroach Janta Party got its minister. The students got their meme. And the exam system got what it always gets — another committee that'll outlive us all."
