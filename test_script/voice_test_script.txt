Here's a number that should worry you: one point three lakh. That's how many MBBS seats exist in the entire country, for every student who wants to become a doctor.

The National Testing Agency was created in November 2017 with one job — run India's biggest exam without breaking it. Nine years later, ninety-nine percent of that promise is still, technically, a work in progress.

So why does this keep happening? Is it the exam? Is it the agency? Or is it just that we've built a system where twenty-two point seven lakh people compete for one point three lakh seats, and called that fair?

Whole towns exist because of this math. Kota, in Rajasthan, is basically one giant coaching economy — parents relocate their teenagers for a year, sometimes two, betting a serious chunk of household savings on one exam.

A judge once compared frustrated activists to cockroaches. Within seventy-eight hours, they'd turned it into a political party. That's not a footnote — that's the whole country's sense of humor, weaponized.

Whatever happens next, one thing is certain: the committee report is already being written. It always is.
